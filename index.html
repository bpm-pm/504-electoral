    How precincts are scored

    Each candidate's share in a precinct is compared with that candidate's own average across every precinct in their race, so a +5 pts precinct beat the candidate's usual result by 5 points. A precinct's score is the average of those gaps across every listed candidate who was on its ballot. Raw shares aren't comparable across races (a 3-way council race and a 2-way runoff differ), which is why the ranking uses the gap rather than the share.

    Avg share and avg votes are per candidate in that precinct. Election-day votes only; early votes have no precinct.

    Boundaries: City of New Orleans GIS, Voting Precincts (349 current precincts). Results: Louisiana Secretary of State, Voter Portal, official precinct returns. Results from 2022–2024 are drawn on today's precinct boundaries. Districts: precinct lists from the most recent SOS races for each district (House districts 2024–2026, Council 2025, Congress 2026, School Board 2024, with districts 1, 3, 5, 6 and 7 rebuilt from the 2020 races and so approximate).

      Where the working class lives: renters, cost burden, low income, wage work, poverty and race

      The four parishes

      American Community Survey 2020–2024 five-year estimates. Metro is the four parishes combined. The map and table compare places against each other; the parish or metro figure is the benchmark.

      All areas

      Sources. U.S. Census Bureau, American Community Survey 2020–2024 5-year estimates, retrieved through Census Reporter: tenure (B25003), housing costs as a share of income (B25140, B25070), household income (B19001, B19013), ratio of income to poverty (C17002), class of worker (B24080), race and Hispanic origin (B03002), employment status (B23025). Voter registration by party: Louisiana Secretary of State parish report of registered voters, Oct. 1, 2026 (Democrat, Republican, and no party plus other parties); registration is counted by precinct and shared out to tracts and ZIP codes by the share of each precinct's area they cover. Census tracts cover Orleans, Jefferson, St. Bernard and St. Tammany parishes; tract boundaries: Census TIGER 2024. ZIP codes are ZIP Code Tabulation Areas; State House figures are for the whole district, including any part outside Orleans Parish.

      Estimated districts. City Council, School Board and congressional figures are for Orleans Parish only. Census does not publish City Council or School Board districts; their figures here add up census tracts, splitting tracts that cross a line by the share of their area on each side. Congressional figures are the Orleans Parish part of each district on the 120th Congress lines.

      Reading the numbers. Tract estimates come from samples and carry wide margins of error; areas with fewer than 300 households are marked with a dot (•) and left out of the working-class index. The index compares each area with census tracts in its own parish, so a Jefferson tract scores against Jefferson tracts. The poverty line scales with household size, so "below 200% of poverty" already compares income to need for each family size. Wage and salary workers include private, nonprofit and government employees; self-employed includes owners of incorporated and unincorporated businesses.

      Other sources. The Bureau of Labor Statistics publishes unemployment for Orleans Parish as a whole (LAUS), not for neighborhoods, so the unemployment rate here is the ACS five-year figure. The Data Center publishes neighborhood profiles and maps of New Orleans that are useful alongside this page.

      How the scores work. Working class and DSA vote are turned into percentiles against the other precincts in the same parish, so 50 is a typical precinct for its parish. Membership is ranked across all four parishes together, so a district with a handful of members scores low wherever it is. A district that crosses parish lines averages its precincts' percentiles. City Council and School Board are Orleans-only. Suburban precincts only have results for the 2022–2026 PSC, BESE, Congress and DSCC races (plus the 2026 Abita Springs mayor's race), so their DSA vote scores rest on fewer candidates. Working class is the Demographics working-class index for the census tracts each precinct overlaps. DSA vote is the Trends & data precinct score: how far DSA candidates (or DSA + recommended) ran above their own citywide average there. Membership is members in good standing per 10,000 residents. Members are only known by ZIP code, so a precinct takes the rate of its ZIP. District scores use the actual member counts per district from the upcoming-races sheet, except Congress, which uses precincts. The run score is the plain average of the three. It is a starting point for a conversation, not a verdict: it can't see candidates, money, or the incumbent.
